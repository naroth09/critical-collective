An archive built against the loss of writing on art in India.

Critical Collective is an initiative by the curator and critic Gayatri Sinha. It edits, commissions and preserves scholarship on the visual arts of the subcontinent — and keeps it in reach.

Why it exists

India can claim a history of outstanding Indological research and publication from the early 19th century. The present scenario in writing on the visual arts is bleak.

Major texts on Indian art history, aesthetics, iconography and modern and contemporary art lapse from the public sphere once they are out of print. Very few new studies or anthologies are commissioned. Since its inception Critical Collective has collaborated with institutions in India and abroad on exhibitions and seminars; the website took that work to a larger scale.

It is built on the need for concerted editing and preservation, and it deliberately addresses categories with little significant writing at present — lens based practices, video and photography. As repositories holding rare books on art shrink, the loss of knowledge in the arts is a real concern.

Objectives

One

Provide discourse on Indian visual arts, with access to its recent history and developments.

Two

Make available the forgotten and lesser known histories, in order to locate their contemporaneity.

Three

Offer new ways of interpreting visual arts in India, for the current and future generation.

Director

GS

Gayatri Sinha

A critic, editor and curator working in the domains of gender, lens-based art and cultural politics. In 2017 she received the Tate Asia research fellowship grant, and is currently editing a volume on modern and contemporary photography.
